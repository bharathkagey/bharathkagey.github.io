FRUB Die Development — 200,000 Shot Die Life Program
FRUB HPDC die — 200,000 shot life program, Tesla GIGA Castings

ⓘ All images are sourced from publicly available materials and do not contain any proprietary or confidential company data.

Objective: Redesign the FRUB HPDC die from scratch to extend die life beyond the 140,000 shot predecessor, resolve chronic casting cracking in the motor cover and wheel house regions, and reduce cycle time.

Result: Achieved 200,000 shot die life (+43% over baseline), reduced cycle time from 105 sec to 85 sec (19% improvement), and eliminated chronic cracking defects through integrated architecture, cooling, and gating redesign.

140k → 200k shots (+43%) 105s → 85s cycle time Refurb: weeks → days Zone-by-zone steel coating spec

Technical Approach

  • Introduced a dedicated insert holder with sub-inserted cavity blocks, replacing monolithic inserts with modular sections — each high-wear zone independently replaceable without full die strip, reducing unplanned downtime and extending overall die service life.
  • Engineered conformal and 3D-printed cooling channels using BTU extraction calculations to achieve die face thermal balance, directly driving the 105s → 85s cycle time reduction at Model Y production rates.
  • Redesigned runner and gating system to resolve melt fill deficiencies in motor cover and wheel house areas — controlled solidification front progression to eliminate crack initiation; validated in MAGMASOFT before cutting steel.
  • Defined surface coating treatment and heat treatment parameters zone-by-zone with steel suppliers, targeting surface hardness improvement at high-wear gating and overflow zones — coordinated with BTU cooling layout to reduce thermal cycling stress.
  • Integrated quick-connect 6061 aluminum cooling manifolds and brass couplers to eliminate stainless steel hard plumbing, cutting refurbishment turnaround from weeks to days.
  • Cross-factory knowledge exchange with Giga Berlin engineering team — captured lessons learned from their FRUB die experience and incorporated proven improvement trials into the new design baseline.
  • Full die assembly validated in MAGMASOFT (flow, thermal, solidification) and ANSYS (structural deformation, platen deflection, support pillar preload) prior to first steel cut.

Shock Tower cooling redesign achieved 100s → 90s cycle time reduction and extended insert service intervals.
  • Structural & Fixture: Performed ANSYS structural analysis on cover/ejector halves and DCM platens to size support pillar preload, eliminating flash from die deflection under shot pressure. Designed a 6061 machined aluminum best-fit gauge fixture for Shock Tower castings enabling instant production-floor distortion measurement without CMM setup, accelerating root cause turnaround on quality holds.

Aluminum Manifold & Cooling Coupler Design — Die Refurbishment Optimization
Brass quick-connect coupler with O-ring face seals — plug-and-play cooling interface
Machined 6061/6063 aluminum manifold body — standardized HPDC die cooling interface
Cooling circuit manifold assembly — replacing stainless hard plumbing with quick-connect system

ⓘ All images are sourced from publicly available materials and do not contain any proprietary or confidential company data.

Objective: Eliminate stainless steel hard plumbing with compression fittings — identified as the primary driver of 6-week die refurbishment cycles on GIGA dies.

Result: Die refurbishment turnaround reduced from 6 weeks to 2 weeks (67% reduction). Design subsequently adopted as the standard cooling interface on the FRUB die development program.

6 weeks → 2 weeks refurb 67% downtime reduction Adopted on FRUB program

Technical Approach

  • Design: Machined 6061/6063 Al manifold bodies with brass quick-connect couplers and O-ring face seals — replacing stainless steel hard plumbing with a standardized plug-and-play interface rated for HPDC cooling circuit pressures and temperatures. Design accounted for differential thermal expansion between the Al manifold and die steel body, swarf contamination ingress, and field-replaceable sealing elements.
  • End-to-End Ownership: Produced all machining drawings, managed fabrication, led die installation, and trained technicians on connection procedure. Trialed on Die S to validate sealing performance under production conditions before implementing on Die Q.
  • Impact & Adoption: Reduced refurbishment turnaround from 6 weeks to 2 weeks by eliminating custom plumbing rework on every die swap. Technology adopted as the standard cooling interface on the FRUB die development program — demonstrating cross-program impact beyond original scope.
Standards Development · GD&T · ENOVIA/PDM
GD&T Standards Development — Tesla GIGA Die Families
GD&T datum scheme framework — three-plane structure for GIGA die inserts
RSS statistical tolerance analysis — worst-case vs. probable assembly variation

ⓘ All images are sourced from publicly available materials and do not contain any proprietary or confidential company data.

Objective: Establish a unified GD&T and datum scheme standard for Tesla GIGA die tooling, balancing three competing demands: in-production thermal expansion, flash prevention at parting surfaces, and insert interchangeability during refurbishment.

Result: Standard adopted as governing reference in ENOVIA/PLM for all GIGA die releases. Saved 2 hours per insert replacement in the tool shop and reduced supplier drawing interpretation errors during die buy-off.

2 hrs saved per insert swap RSS statistical method Governing ENOVIA reference

Technical Approach

  • Standard Scope: Authored GD&T and datum scheme standard balancing three competing functional demands simultaneously — thermal expansion compensation across HPDC cycling temperatures (~200°C delta), flash prevention at parting surface interfaces under 6200-ton clamp force, and insert-to-holder fit tolerances enabling drop-in replacement without hand fitting or shimming.
  • Tolerance Method: Applied RSS (Root Sum Square) statistical tolerance analysis to determine probable assembly variation across the die insert stack, avoiding unnecessarily tight tolerances that inflate supplier machining cost with no quality benefit over worst-case arithmetic stackup. Output directly sized locating features and interface clearances.
  • Deployment: Integrated standard into ENOVIA/PLM as the governing reference for all GIGA die releases across Lathrop and Fremont. Consistent supplier drawing interpretation reduced revision cycles at die buy-off; defined insert datum schemes saved 2 hrs per replacement in the tool shop by eliminating fit-and-adjust.

20–50 drawings per die set Up to 2 hrs → instant Deployed to supplier releases

Technical Approach

  • Pipeline Architecture: Built a 3D-to-2D drawing automation pipeline using PowerShell scripting against the CATIA V5 COM API, with AI-assisted code generation (Claude) to develop and iterate scripts against specific engineering requirements. The pipeline reads sub-insert CAD geometry, extracts view projections, and auto-populates drawing templates with dimensional and annotation data.
  • Engineering Requirements: Defined all automation logic against the internally developed GIGA die GD&T standard — datum scheme placement, feature control frame callouts, tolerance values by insert zone, title block field population, and revision table logic. Output covers sub-insert production drawings and ignition schematics: 20–50 drawings per die set.
  • Validation & Deployment: Deployed pipeline output directly to supplier drawing releases — output met Tesla's production drawing quality bar, validating this as an engineering deliverable pipeline rather than an internal productivity tool. Per-drawing turnaround reduced from up to 2 hours to near-instant generation.
